\section*{Part 1: Exploring Sizing Tradeoffs Using SA}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{2) Device type for sinking current:} Sinking current means we use NMOS devices.
    \item \textbf{3) Required $\lambda$:} The spec needs a $< 10\%$ change in current for a $1\text{V}$ change in output. Since $\frac{\Delta I}{I} \approx \lambda \Delta V_{DS}$, for $\Delta V_{DS} = 1\text{V}$ and $\frac{\Delta I}{I} \le 0.1$, the required $\lambda \le 0.1 \text{ V}^{-1}$.
    \item \textbf{4) Trade-offs Table (Fixed total area):}
    \begin{table}[h!]
        \centering
        \begin{tabular}{lcc}
            \toprule
            \textbf{Parameter} & \textbf{Higher $g_m/I_D$ (lower $V^*$)} & \textbf{Lower $g_m/I_D$ (higher $V^*$)} \\
            \midrule
            Dependence on $V_{DS}$ ($\lambda$) & Higher & Lower \\
            Random Mismatch & Higher & Lower \\
            Systematic Mismatch & Higher & Lower \\
            Compliance Voltage & Lower (Better) & Higher \\
            \bottomrule
        \end{tabular}
    \end{table}
    \item \textbf{6) Comparing $\sigma_{VT}$ from the model vs. ADT:}
    From the model file, \texttt{par\_vth = 0.007148}. This is the mismatch between two identical transistors. For a single transistor, $\sigma_{VT\_model} = 0.007148 \times 0.7071 \approx 5.05\text{mV}$.
    From ADT, \texttt{vgmis = 5.131mV}. The ADT value is slightly higher because it's estimated from a finite Monte Carlo simulation, so it includes random variations.
    \item \textbf{8) Is highest $V^*$ desirable?} Yes, pushing for the highest $V^*$ lowers mismatch, takes up less area, and improves $\lambda$. It's only limited by the headroom (compliance voltage).
    \item \textbf{11) Selected design point ($V^* = 150\text{mV}$):} $L = 1.415\mu\text{m}$, Area $= 20.67\text{p}$, $\lambda = 0.0397$. This satisfies the constraint $\lambda < 0.1$.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Part 2: Current Mirror Simulation}
\subsection*{1. Design and OP Analysis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{1.1) Rough value for $R_B$:} For a $50\text{mV}$ saturation margin, $V_{DS2} \approx V^* + 50\text{mV} = 200\text{mV}$. $R_B = 200\text{mV} / 10\mu\text{A} = 20\text{k}\Omega$.
    \item \textbf{1.2) Selected $R_B$ size:} The simulated $R_B$ needs to be larger than $20\text{k}\Omega$ because the rough calculation ignored the body effect on M4, which increases its $V_{TH}$ and requires a larger voltage drop across $R_B$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{2. DC Sweep ($I_{out}$ vs $V_{OUT}$)}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{2.1) Differences \& Compliance Voltage:} The Wide Swing mirror has a flatter current curve ($R_{out}$ is higher) but enters saturation later ($\approx 350\text{mV}$) compared to the simple mirror ($\approx 150\text{mV}$). $I_{out} = 2 I_B$ exactly when $V_{OUT} = V_{GS1}$, where channel-length modulation is perfectly canceled.
    \item \textbf{2.4) $R_{out}$ change with $V_{OUT}$:} $R_{out}$ is not perfectly constant because $\lambda$ is non-linear. As $V_{DS}$ increases, the effective channel length decreases dynamically.
    \item \textbf{2.5) Analytical $R_{out}$:} Simple: $R_{out} \approx r_{o2}$. Wide Swing: $R_{out} \approx g_{m4} r_{o4} r_{o2}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{3. Mismatch}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{3.5) More pronounced mismatch:} The mismatch in the bottom current mirror devices is much more pronounced. They act as a common-source amplifier for the error voltage, while the cascode device acts like a common-gate stage heavily degenerated by the bottom transistor's $r_o$.
    \item \textbf{3.6) Better design decision:} It's better to use larger W and L for the bottom current mirror devices and save area on the cascode devices, because cascode mismatch barely affects the output current.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{4. Monte Carlo Simulation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{4.3 \& 4.4) $\sigma(I_{out})/I_{out}$:} Simple Current Mirror error is $2.10\%$. Wide Swing Current Mirror error is $1.86\%$. Both have similar standard deviations ($\approx 0.4\mu\text{A}$), but the wide swing centers closer to the ideal $20\mu\text{A}$ mean due to better suppression of systematic offsets.
\end{itemize}
